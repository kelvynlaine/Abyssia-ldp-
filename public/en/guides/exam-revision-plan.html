Exam revision plan: a 4-week schedule

By Kelvyn Laine, creator of Abyssia · updated October 30, 2026

In short: To revise for a big exam in four weeks, first list every chapter and rate your level on each one, then spread them out starting with the weakest, come back to each chapter several times a few days apart, and keep the last week for full papers under real exam conditions.

Step 1: take stock

List, subject by subject, every chapter on the exam syllabus. Next to each one, note your level: green (I've got it), orange (needs work), red (I don't know it). This table stops you revising at random and spending too much time on what you already know.

Step 2: spread it over four weeks

  1. Week 1: the red chapters. Go back to the lesson, make one revision sheet per chapter, and end each session with a short quiz.
  2. Week 2: the orange chapters, plus a first quick review of the week 1 chapters.
  3. Week 3: the green chapters and typical exam exercises, with a second review of weeks 1 and 2.
  4. Week 4: full past papers under timed conditions, marked, then a final review of the concepts that are still shaky.

Why come back to each chapter several times

Revising a chapter once, even for a long time, is less effective than coming back to it several times a few days apart. This is the spacing effect, one of the most robust findings in memory research. In the plan above, every weak chapter is seen at least three times before the exam.

A typical revision day

  • Three or four work blocks of 25 to 50 minutes, with breaks (see the Pomodoro technique).
  • Two different subjects in the day rather than just one: alternating stops you burning out.
  • Every block ends with a test: a quiz, flashcards or an exercise without the answer key.
  • One free evening a week, and full nights of sleep: sleep plays a part in memory (see revising the night before an exam).

Keeping track of your plan

Each evening, write down what you did and move chapters from red to orange, then to green.
