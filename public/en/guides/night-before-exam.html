Revising the night before an exam: what to do (and avoid)

By Kelvyn Laine, creator of Abyssia · updated October 30, 2026

In short: The night before an exam, don't learn new chapters: go over your notes and past mistakes, do a quick test to reassure yourself, get your things ready, then stop early and sleep. A full night's sleep helps your memory more than an all-nighter of revision.

What to do

  1. Go over your notes and mind maps, chapter by chapter, trying to recite before you read.
  2. Review your mistakes: the flashcards you missed, the quiz questions you got wrong, the comments on marked work.
  3. Take a short test (15 to 20 minutes) on the most important concepts, to check they are there.
  4. Get your things ready: exam slip, ID, pens, an approved calculator, a watch, water.
  5. Stop early and do something calm before going to bed.

What to avoid

  • Starting a new chapter: it is unlikely to stick and it adds to the stress.
  • Pulling an all-nighter: lack of sleep reduces attention and memory on exam day.
  • Comparing your revision with other people's on social media.
  • Too much caffeine or energy drinks, which make it harder to fall asleep.

Why sleep matters

During sleep, the brain consolidates what you learned during the day. The links between sleep and memory are well documented: a good night before the exam is worth more than a few extra hours of revision. It's also why revision should be spread over several weeks (see the four-week revision plan).

On the morning of the exam

  • A normal breakfast, without changing your habits.
  • A five-minute glance at a summary sheet, no more.
  • Arrive early so you don't have to rush.
  • During the exam: read the whole paper, start with what you know best, and keep time to check your answers.
