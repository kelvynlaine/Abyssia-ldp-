How to study with AI: a 5-step method

By Kelvyn Laine, creator of Abyssia · updated October 30, 2026

In short: To study with AI, first have it explain the chapter, then test yourself with quizzes and flashcards generated from that lesson, and repeat those tests over several days. The AI produces the material and the questions; the learning comes from testing yourself, not from rereading.

Why AI genuinely helps you revise

Generative AI produces in seconds what used to take hours to prepare: a structured lesson, worked examples, practice questions. The point is not to let the AI do the studying for you, but to have something to test yourself on straight away. And self-testing is one of the best-researched memory techniques: the testing effect shows that retrieving information from memory strengthens it more than reading it again.

The 5-step method

  1. Have the chapter explained. Give the AI the subject, the chapter and your level (for example "linear functions, year 11"). Ask for a full lesson with definitions, key properties and worked examples.
  2. Check it against your own notes. Compare with your teacher's notes or the textbook. AI can make mistakes: what counts for the exam is the syllabus and what was covered in class.
  3. Test yourself with a quiz. Generate a multiple-choice quiz from the topic or by pasting in your notes. Answer without looking, then read the explanations for the questions you got wrong.
  4. Turn mistakes into flashcards. Every concept you missed becomes a question-and-answer card to review over the following days (see flashcards and spaced repetition).
  5. Plan short sessions. Study in 25-minute blocks with a break in between (see the Pomodoro technique for studying) and come back to each chapter several days apart.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Copying an AI answer without understanding it: you won't have the AI in the exam room.
  • Rereading the generated lesson over and over instead of quizzing yourself on it.
  • Cramming everything the night before: memory holds on far better to revision spread over several days.
  • Never checking: an AI can invent a date, a formula or a reference. Verify the facts that matter.
