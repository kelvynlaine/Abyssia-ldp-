Learning foreign language vocabulary: the method that works

By Kelvyn Laine, creator of Abyssia · updated October 30, 2026

In short: To remember vocabulary, learn words inside a sentence rather than on their own, review them with flashcards in both directions (your language to the target language and back), spread your reviews over several days, and use each word in speaking or writing as soon as you can.

Learn words in context

A word on its own is quickly forgotten. Note it down with an example sentence, the article or gender for nouns (le, la, der, die, el…), the preposition that goes with it for verbs, and a word from the same family. The Spanish "acordarse de" sticks better with "No me acuerdo de su nombre" than with the bare translation "to remember".

Two-way flashcards

  • Target language to English: to understand a text or a conversation.
  • English to target language: harder, but it's what you need to speak and write.
  • Say the word out loud before turning the card over, to work on pronunciation too.
  • A small amount every day (10 to 20 new words) beats 100 words the night before the test.

Space out your reviews

Review a new word the same day, the next day, then a few days later and again a week after that. Words you know well come back rarely; words you miss come back quickly. This is spaced repetition, explained in detail in our guide to flashcards.

Put the words to use

  1. Write three sentences a day using the previous day's words.
  2. Read or listen to something short in the language (an article, a song, a video) and spot the words you've learned.
  3. Speak: with a classmate, a pen pal or a conversational AI, trying to fit in the new words.
  4. Group words by theme (travel, work, feelings) so you can call on them together.

Why this works better than lists

Reading a long word list gives the feeling of learning, but recognising a word is not the same as being able to produce it. Every step above makes you retrieve the word yourself, in a real context, which is what you'll have to do in an oral exam or a conversation.
